\opening{Dear Editor,}

Please consider our manuscript \emph{``\PTITLE''} for publication in \emph{Neurocomputing} as a regular research article.

\textbf{Type of contribution.} The paper contributes to the understanding of neural network architectures rather than proposing a new model. Selective state-space models (Mamba and its successors) are widely credited with robustness because of their input-dependent selectivity. We test this attribution with a pre-specified freeze--remove--add ablation chain on a Mamba-3 block, using cross-condition bearing-vibration data under matched additive noise as a controlled testbed. Making the scan's dynamics and read/write coefficients input-independent leaves the block's advantage over a time-invariant SSM intact, including under independent training and evaluation noise; the multiplicative output gate contributes most at the deepest noise level, and an additive branch of identical size does not substitute for it. The study therefore speaks directly to the journal's interest in architectures and the analysis of network behaviour; the vibration data serve as a testbed, not as the object of the contribution.

\textbf{Rigour and transparency.} Decision thresholds were written before each grid was run; for the later grids the pre-specification was committed to a public repository before the first run. Reporting at the final epoch is primary, and all data, code and decision records are publicly available.

\textbf{Related submission.} A related manuscript by the corresponding author and a co-author, ``When does a second vibration sensor help? A controlled case study of sensor configuration for deep-learning bearing fault diagnosis'', is under consideration at \emph{Measurement Science and Technology} (MST-140500, submitted 8 October 2026). It addresses sensor-configuration choices and shares the experimental platform, but makes no claims about the component attribution studied here; no figure, table or result is shared between the two manuscripts. An auxiliary kinematics-alignment loss that appeared in early versions of the model plays no role in the present conclusions; all attribution arms are trained with cross-entropy only.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission. Generative-AI use is declared in the manuscript and in Appendix~C.
