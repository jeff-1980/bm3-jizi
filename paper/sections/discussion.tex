\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What can be credited?} The ablation chain supports a narrow, conditional statement: within this Mamba-3 block, on this testbed and with noise-aware training, (i) making the scan fully input-independent---first the $\Delta$, $A$, $B$, $C$ projections, then the trapezoidal weight and rotation angles as well---did not remove the block's deep-noise advantage over a time-invariant S4D, and at the deepest level the fully frozen arm matched or exceeded the original block with fewer parameters than BM3 or BM3-frozen (though more than S4D), (ii) removing the output gate removed most of that advantage---(i) and (ii) holding on the reverse transition as well, where under independent noise gate removal is resolved at every level---and (iii) adding the gate to S4D recovered part of the advantage only together with added width and projections, and an additive branch of the same size recovered as much, while (iv) in the Mamba-3 block an additive branch of the same size could not be distinguished from removing the gate, on both transitions. Under independent training and evaluation noise (Section~\ref{sec:results:indep}), (i) holds, (ii) is resolved only at $-6$\,dB, and in the fully frozen block the gate carries the advantage at $-6$\,dB but not at $0$ and $-2$\,dB. These observations are consistent with a gate benefit that is specific to the multiplicative operator in its native host and grows with noise depth. They do not identify which other component the gate depends on. Nor do they separate ``input-dependence contributes nothing here'' from ``what it contributes is recovered by learned constants under this budget''; the two readings are indistinguishable in our data and lead to the same attribution. Representability results for selectivity~\cite{huang2025selectivity} are not contradicted by a single ablation on one testbed. The S4D-superiority finding~\cite{saadatmand2026s4d} was obtained on clean classification benchmarks, without matched noise or a shift of operating condition, so it does not speak to the regime studied here and we do not read it as covering it. Two points of contact are nevertheless consistent with it: our clean-data parity between S4D and the Mamba-style arms, and our finding that input-dependent selectivity is not needed for the block's advantage. What this paper adds is specific to the matched-noise, cross-condition regime: there S4D trails the frozen Mamba-3 block by $12$--$28$$\pp$ under final-epoch reporting and independent training and evaluation noise (partial and full freeze, both transitions), and the gap does not require input-dependent scan coefficients; at the deepest level it depends on the multiplicative output gate. This is a statement about a different regime, not a contradiction of the clean-benchmark result.

\textbf{Implications for backbone selection and for transplant practice.} For practitioners choosing backbones for noisy, drifting signal regimes, the results suggest that the block's benefit should be judged as a composition, and that a gate added to a different host should not be expected to reproduce it: in our S4D host a gate built in the donor's shape recovered no more than an additive branch of the same size on the original transition (Section~\ref{sec:results:graftwm}) and only slightly more on the reverse one (Section~\ref{sec:results:reverse}), and neither design was tuned. A hypothesis for future work follows: a recomposed block guided by the component map (no conv stem, gate retained, S4D-style $A$) may match or exceed the original on this testbed; we leave it untested, because post-hoc recomposition after seeing the map would not carry the evidential status of the pre-specified chain.

\textbf{Why might gating be composition-bound?} We refrain from a mechanistic claim beyond the data, but note three compatible observations. First, the gate-related differences concentrate on the minority, lowest-recall class (Section~\ref{sec:results:perclass}), and matched-noise training lifts that class \emph{only when the gate is present}. One hypothesis, which we did not test, is that the gate is the component through which matched-noise training improves hard-class recall, i.e., that it exploits noise-aware training rather than acting as a test-time denoiser; we measured neither classification margins nor gate activations, and the reading is in any case bounded by the matched-SNR scope (Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}). Second, the multiplicative form admits a per-sample suppression interpretation, but no gate statistics were recorded, so this remains a hypothesis rather than an observation. Third, the gateless composition falling to or below S4D at the deepest noise levels is compatible with the other components being calibrated to a gated regime (e.g., the stem may pass features that are only safe when the gate can veto them); this is the weakest of the three observations, since the gateless--S4D gap is not resolved with five seeds (Section~\ref{sec:results:sensitivity}). All are testable in future work via pairwise interaction grids and mismatched-noise protocols.
