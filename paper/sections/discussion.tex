\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What can be credited?} The ablation chain supports a narrow, conditional statement: within this Mamba-3 block, on this testbed and with noise-aware training, (i) making the scan fully input-independent---first the $\Delta$, $A$, $B$, $C$ projections, then the trapezoidal weight and rotation angles as well---did not remove the block's deep-noise advantage over a time-invariant S4D, and at the deepest level the fully frozen arm matched or exceeded the original block with fewer parameters, (ii) removing the output gate removed most of that advantage---(i) and (ii) holding on the reverse transition as well---and (iii) adding the gate to S4D recovered part of the advantage only together with added width and projections, and an additive branch of the same size recovered as much, while (iv) in the Mamba-3 block an additive branch of the same size recovered none of what removing the gate cost. These observations are consistent with a gate benefit that is specific to the multiplicative operator in its native host. They do not identify which other component the gate depends on. Nor do they separate ``input-dependence contributes nothing here'' from ``what it contributes is recovered by learned constants under this budget''; the two readings are indistinguishable in our data and lead to the same attribution. Representability results for selectivity~\cite{huang2025selectivity} and the S4D-superiority finding on clean data~\cite{saadatmand2026s4d} are not contradicted by a single ablation on one testbed; our clean-data parity agrees with the latter, and our deep-noise result suggests that in this block the output gate, rather than any input-dependent term of the scan, accounts for most of the measured advantage.

\textbf{Implications for backbone selection and for transplant practice.} For practitioners choosing backbones for noisy, drifting signal regimes, the results suggest that the block's benefit should be judged as a composition, and that a gate added to a different host should not be expected to reproduce it: in our S4D host a gate built in the donor's shape recovered no more than an additive branch of the same size (Section~\ref{sec:results:graftwm}), and neither design was tuned. A hypothesis for future work follows: a recomposed block guided by the component map (no conv stem, gate retained, S4D-style $A$) may match or exceed the original on this testbed; we leave it untested, because post-hoc recomposition after seeing the map would not carry the evidential status of the pre-specified chain.

\textbf{Why might gating be composition-bound?} We refrain from a mechanistic claim beyond the data, but note three compatible observations. First, the gate-related differences concentrate on the minority, lowest-recall class (Section~\ref{sec:results:perclass}), and matched-noise training lifts that class \emph{only when the gate is present}---suggesting the gate is the component through which matched-noise training is converted into hard-class margin, i.e., an exploiter of noise-aware training rather than a test-time denoiser (this reading is bounded by the matched-SNR scope, Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}). Second, its role appears closer to adaptive per-sample feature suppression than to generic denoising. Third, the gateless composition falling to or below S4D at the deepest noise levels is compatible with the other components being calibrated to a gated regime (e.g., the stem may pass features that are only safe when the gate can veto them); this is the weakest of the three observations, since the gateless--S4D gap is not resolved with five seeds (Section~\ref{sec:results:sensitivity}). All are testable in future work via pairwise interaction grids and mismatched-noise protocols.
