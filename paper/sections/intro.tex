\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Selective state-space models (SSMs) of the Mamba family have rapidly become default sequence backbones, and a common narrative has formed around \emph{why} they work: input-dependent selectivity---the ability to modulate state dynamics per token---is credited with filtering noise, adapting to distribution shift, and outperforming time-invariant predecessors such as S4D~\cite{gu2023mamba}. \cite{qu2024mambasurvey,patro2024mamba360} This belief motivates a growing family of ``selective'' architectures, yet it sits in unresolved tension with recent evidence: the first systematic S4D-versus-Mamba comparison on time-series classification finds the \emph{simpler, time-invariant} model consistently superior~\cite{saadatmand2026s4d}, and theoretical analyses identify expressivity regimes where Mamba is provably weaker~\cite{yu2025blockbiased}. Which account is right---and, more importantly, \emph{what exactly should be credited} when a Mamba-style block does prove robust? This paper addresses the second question for one block and one testbed, with a controlled ablation chain whose decision thresholds were fixed before each grid was run. The testbed is bearing vibration diagnosis under operating-condition drift and matched noise, in which the robustness gap is large (up to 18$\pp$), the evaluation labels are identical across all 865 cells (hash-verified), and the block can be dissected.

Our approach is deliberately \emph{attributional} rather than \emph{methodological}: we propose no new architecture and claim no state-of-the-art. Instead we take a regime in which a Mamba-style block exhibits a large, stable robustness advantage over a time-invariant SSM, and ask which ingredient earns it. The chain has three steps, each with adjudication thresholds fixed before the corresponding grid was run:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Test the classical selectivity terms (necessity test).} We construct a \emph{frozen} arm: the input-dependent parts of $\Delta$, $A$, $B$ and $C$ are replaced by learned constants. The Mamba-3 scan also takes an input-dependent trapezoidal weight and input-dependent rotation angles; these, the convolutional stem, the multiplicative SiLU gate, the state parameterisation, normalisation and projections are retained (Section~\ref{sec:protocol:freeze}). If these four terms drive robustness, the frozen arm should collapse toward the time-invariant baseline.
\item \textbf{Localise the necessary component.} On the frozen base, we remove one component at a time (convolutional stem; SiLU gate; heavy-tailed state parameterisation) under a single-variable discipline checked key-by-key on the state dictionary (Section~\ref{sec:protocol:freeze}), and adjudicate each arm against pre-specified $\Delta$-thresholds.
\item \textbf{Test sufficiency by constructive grafting.} The component identified as necessary is added to plain S4D as a gate branch, and a pre-specified recovery ratio decides whether ``removal collapses'' is matched by ``addition restores''.
\end{enumerate}

The outcome is a conditional pattern (final-epoch reporting; best-epoch values in Section~\ref{sec:results:sensitivity}). Freezing the input-dependent $\Delta$, $A$, $B$, $C$ projections costs at most about $4\pp$ in deep noise (paired intervals include zero) while the block retains a 15--20$\pp$ advantage over S4D: under the pre-specified criterion these projections are not the primary contributor. Freezing the trapezoidal weight and the rotation angles as well---so that nothing entering the scan depends on the input---does not shrink the advantage either: it measures $+21$ to $+24\pp$ over S4D with fewer parameters (Section~\ref{sec:results:fullfreeze}). Removing the SiLU gate costs 9--23$\pp$ and takes the block to roughly the level of plain S4D. Adding a gate branch to S4D recovers only $R = 0.20$ of the gap ($0.17$ under best-epoch reporting). Rebuilt in the donor's width and projection structure, the graft recovers $R = 0.30$ (interval $0.16$--$0.52$), but an additive branch with identical parameters recovers at least as much ($0.41$, Section~\ref{sec:results:graftwm}). The converse test closes the argument: replacing the gate inside the Mamba-3 block by an additive branch with identical parameters and initial weights rescues none of the loss on either transition (rescue ratios $0.02$ and $0.01$, Section~\ref{sec:results:nativeadd}). The gate is necessary as a multiplicative operator in its native block; in the S4D host it adds nothing beyond an additive branch of the same size on the original transition and a small increment on the reverse one. The first two steps replicate on the reverse transition (Section~\ref{sec:results:reverse}). A per-class analysis on a separate, shorter grid indicates that the gate-related differences concentrate on the minority class.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) A layered freezing experiment on a Mamba-3 block under distribution drift and matched noise, from the classical selectivity terms ($\Delta$, $A$, $B$, $C$) through to a scan in which no tensor depends on the input, in which the block's deep-noise advantage over S4D survives every step; each arm is verified input-independent by capturing the tensors the scan receives for two different inputs. (ii) Component-level evidence that the output gate is the one ablated component whose removal causes a large, one-signed loss, and that adding it to a time-invariant host, even in the donor's width and with an additive control of identical size, recovers no more than the added structure does, while replacing it in its own block by an additive branch of identical size recovers nothing on either transition; the first two findings also replicate on the reverse transition. (iii) Final-epoch reporting under a fixed budget for every arm of the chain, with best-epoch selection on the evaluation condition as a sensitivity analysis: absolute levels shift by $0.6$--$10.6\pp$ while no contrast changes sign. (iv) A documented pre-specification and review trail---evaluation-label hashes, thresholds written before each grid, and agent-assisted review---including the incidents in which automated adjudication departed from the pre-specified criteria; we release the trail.
