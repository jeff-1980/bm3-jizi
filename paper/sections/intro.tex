\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Selective state-space models (SSMs) of the Mamba family have rapidly become default sequence backbones, and a common narrative has formed around \emph{why} they work: input-dependent selectivity---the ability to modulate state dynamics per token---is credited with filtering noise, adapting to distribution shift, and outperforming time-invariant predecessors such as S4D~\cite{gu2023mamba}. \cite{qu2024mambasurvey,patro2024mamba360} This belief motivates a growing family of ``selective'' architectures, yet it sits in unresolved tension with recent evidence: the first systematic S4D-versus-Mamba comparison on time-series classification finds the \emph{simpler, time-invariant} model consistently superior~\cite{saadatmand2026s4d}, and theoretical analyses identify expressivity regimes where Mamba is provably weaker~\cite{yu2025blockbiased}. Which account is right---and, more importantly, \emph{what exactly should be credited} when a Mamba-style block does prove robust? This paper answers the second question with a controlled, preregistered falsification chain, using bearing vibration diagnosis under operating-condition drift and matched noise as a testbed that makes the robustness gap large (up to 18$\pp$), reproducible (fingerprint-verified across 865 preregistered cells), and dissectible.

Our approach is deliberately \emph{attributional} rather than \emph{methodological}: we propose no new architecture and claim no state-of-the-art. Instead we take a regime in which a Mamba-style block exhibits a large, stable robustness advantage over a time-invariant SSM, and ask which ingredient earns it. The chain has three preregistered steps, each with adjudication thresholds fixed before the corresponding grid was run:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Falsify selectivity (necessity test).} We construct a \emph{frozen-selectivity} arm: the input-dependent projections producing $\Delta$, $B$, $C$ are replaced by learned constants, with every other component of the block---the convolutional stem, the multiplicative SiLU gate, the state parameterisation, normalisation, and projections---left byte-identical. If selectivity drives robustness, the frozen arm should collapse toward the time-invariant baseline.
\item \textbf{Localise the necessary component.} On the frozen base, we remove one component at a time (convolutional stem; SiLU gate; heavy-tailed state parameterisation) under a single-variable discipline verified key-by-key on the state dictionary, and adjudicate each arm against preregistered $\Delta$-thresholds.
\item \textbf{Test sufficiency by constructive grafting.} The component identified as necessary is transplanted onto plain S4D, and a preregistered recovery ratio decides whether ``removal collapses'' is matched by ``addition restores''.
\end{enumerate}

The outcome is a pattern we summarise as \emph{necessary but not transplantable}. Freezing selectivity costs only 2--5$\pp$ in deep noise while the frozen block retains a 14--18$\pp$ advantage over S4D---selectivity is not the mechanism. Removing the SiLU gate is catastrophic: the gateless block falls \emph{below} plain S4D. Yet grafting the same gate onto S4D recovers only $R = 0.17$ of the gap, with essentially zero recovery at the deepest adjudicated noise level. The two results are symmetric and jointly decisive: the gate functions only inside its native composition. Deep-noise robustness is therefore an interaction property of the block, not of any single part. Per-class analysis adds a final layer: the interaction effect concentrates on the hard minority class, whose recall trajectory under noise is governed by gate presence in both ablation directions.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) A preregistered, fingerprint-verified falsification of the selectivity-robustness attribution for Mamba-style blocks under distribution drift and noise, reconciling the apparent conflict between selectivity-credit narratives and recent S4D-superiority findings~\cite{saadatmand2026s4d}: both are consistent once robustness is credited to block composition rather than selectivity. (ii) A component-level attribution protocol---confound elimination (capacity, normalisation, auxiliary losses), frozen-selectivity construction at the module level without kernel modification, single-variable component removal, and constructive grafting---that upgrades ``which architecture wins'' comparisons into ``which mechanism earns it'' analyses. (iii) Evidence that the operative component (multiplicative gating) is necessary but not transplantable, with the effect concentrated on the hard class---a caution for the growing practice of transplanting Mamba components into other backbones expecting robustness gains. (iv) A reproducibility protocol combining evaluation-set fingerprints, preregistered adjudication thresholds, and dual-agent review, under which no post-hoc criterion change survived; we release the full adjudication trail.
