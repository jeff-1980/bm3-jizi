\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this Mamba-3 block, on one controlled drift $\times$ noise testbed with matched-noise training, freezing the input-dependent $\Delta$, $A$, $B$, $C$ projections costs at most about $4\pp$ (an estimate that five seeds leave imprecise) while $15$--$20\pp$ of advantage over a time-invariant SSM remains, and freezing the trapezoidal weight and rotation angles as well---leaving no input-dependent tensor in the scan---costs nothing measurable and leaves $21$--$24\pp$ of advantage with fewer parameters. The multiplicative output gate is the one ablated component whose removal causes a large, one-signed loss ($9$--$23\pp$, returning the block to roughly the level of plain S4D), yet adding the gate to S4D recovers no more than the added structure does: 17--20\% of the gap for a narrow graft, about 30\% for one built in the donor's width, and at least as much for an additive branch of the same size. Conversely, an additive branch of identical size and initial weights, placed where the gate was in the Mamba-3 block, recovers none of the loss on either transition. The gate is thus necessary as a multiplicative operator in its native host, and what transfers to a time-invariant host is added structure rather than gating; the gate-related differences concentrate on the minority class. The first two steps replicate on the reverse transition and on Paderborn bearings when bearing identity is shared, but not on disjoint Paderborn bearings, where no examined architecture transfers. The evidence rests on cross-condition drift with shared bearing identity, five seeds, and final-epoch reporting under a fixed budget, with the earlier evaluation-condition epoch selection retained as a sensitivity analysis. We offer the three-step chain---freeze, remove, add---as a protocol for turning architecture comparisons into component-level tests, with the caveat that the interventions must be matched in width and capacity for the add step to be conclusive, and we release our adjudication trail, including the incidents in which automated adjudication departed from the pre-specified criteria.
