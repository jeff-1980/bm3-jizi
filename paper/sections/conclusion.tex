\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Input-dependent selectivity does not earn the deep-noise robustness of Mamba-style blocks on a controlled drift $\times$ noise testbed: freezing it costs $2$--$5\pp$ while $14$--$18\pp$ of advantage over a time-invariant SSM remains. The multiplicative gate is the necessary component---its removal drops the block below plain S4D---yet it is not transplantable: grafted onto S4D it recovers 17\% of the gap, and essentially nothing at the deepest adjudicated noise level. Robustness here is a property of the composition, concentrated on the hard class, and earned by interaction rather than by any single mechanism. We offer the three-step chain---freeze, remove, graft---as a reusable protocol for turning architecture comparisons into component attributions, and our preregistered adjudication trail as evidence that such attributions can be made resistant to post-hoc rationalisation.
