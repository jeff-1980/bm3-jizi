\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this Mamba-3 block, on one controlled drift $\times$ noise testbed with matched-noise training, freezing the input-dependent $\Delta$, $A$, $B$, $C$ projections costs at most about $5\pp$ (an estimate that five seeds leave imprecise) while $14$--$20\pp$ of advantage over a time-invariant SSM remains; the input-dependent trapezoidal and rotation terms were not tested. The multiplicative output gate is the one ablated component whose removal causes a large, one-signed loss ($9$--$24\pp$, returning the block to roughly the level of plain S4D or below), yet one gate branch added to S4D recovers only 17--20\% of the gap (best-epoch and last-epoch reporting). Because removal and addition are not matched interventions, we read this as a strong host dependence of the gate's benefit in this design, concentrated on the minority class, rather than as a proof of non-transplantability. The evidence rests on one condition pair with four bearings per side, five seeds and an evaluation-condition epoch-selection rule that we re-assessed for four arms. We offer the three-step chain---freeze, remove, add---as a protocol for turning architecture comparisons into component-level tests, with the caveat that the interventions must be matched in width and capacity for the add step to be conclusive, and we release our adjudication trail, including the incidents in which automated adjudication departed from the pre-specified criteria.
