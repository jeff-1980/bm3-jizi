\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this Mamba-3 block, on one controlled shift $\times$ noise testbed with matched-noise training and independent training and evaluation noise, making the scan's dynamics and read/write coefficients input-independent did not remove the block's advantage over a time-invariant S4D: the fully frozen block leads by $+21.3$, $+23.3$, $+20.3$\,pp on the original transition and by $+27.5$, $+26.7$, $+21.1$\,pp on the reverse one. Removing the multiplicative output gate causes a substantial loss at $-6$\,dB, and this cost is resolved only at that level on the original transition (at every level for the partially frozen block on the reverse one; the ranking of the gate against the stem and $A$ ablations comes from the earlier protocol and is descriptive), and without its gate the fully frozen block still exceeds S4D at $0$ and $-2$\,dB. Only in the fully frozen block on the original transition does the gate's contribution grow significantly with noise depth; in the other settings tested the data are compatible with a depth-independent cost. A multiplicative gate exceeds an additive branch of identical size at $-6$\,dB in both hosts, although the pre-specified rescue test is not assessable. On Paderborn the gate-removal cost replicates when bearings are shared between training and evaluation. Grafting a gate onto S4D, per-class results, the stem and $A$ ablations and the disjoint-bearing Paderborn setting were run only under the earlier noise protocol and are descriptive. The evidence rests on one block, one shift pair per dataset, five seeds and final-epoch reporting under a fixed budget. The independent-noise grids contain no unfrozen BM3 arm, so they establish that the frozen block beats S4D, not that freezing preserves the unfrozen block's performance. We offer the chain---freeze, remove, replace---as a protocol for turning architecture comparisons into component-level tests, with two cautions from this study: interventions must be matched in width and capacity, and the noise streams of training and evaluation must be independent.
