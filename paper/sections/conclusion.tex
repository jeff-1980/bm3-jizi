\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this Mamba-3 block, on one controlled shift $\times$ noise testbed with matched-noise training, making the scan's dynamics and read/write coefficients input-independent did not remove the block's advantage over a time-invariant S4D; under independent training and evaluation noise the fully frozen block leads by $+21.3$, $+23.3$, $+20.3$\,pp, and on the reverse transition by $+27.5$, $+26.7$, $+21.1$\,pp. The multiplicative output gate is the ablated component whose removal costs most, but under independent noise this cost is resolved only at $-6$\,dB on the original transition (at every level on the reverse one), and without its gate the fully frozen block still exceeds S4D at milder noise: the gate's contribution grows with noise depth rather than carrying the advantage throughout. An additive branch of identical size, placed where the gate was, could not be distinguished from removing it; added to S4D, a gate recovers part of the gap, not more than an additive branch of the same size on one direction and somewhat more on the other. On Paderborn, the gate-removal cost replicates when bearings are shared between training and evaluation and nothing replicates on disjoint bearings, where every architecture transfers poorly. The evidence rests on one block, one shift pair per dataset, five seeds and final-epoch reporting under a fixed budget. We offer the three-step chain---freeze, remove, replace---as a protocol for turning architecture comparisons into component-level tests, with two cautions from this study: interventions must be matched in width and capacity, and the noise streams of training and evaluation must be independent.
