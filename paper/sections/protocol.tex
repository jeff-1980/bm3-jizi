\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Testbed, arms, and budget}
\label{sec:protocol:testbed}

Vibration windows from the XJTU-SY run-to-failure dataset~\cite{wang2020xjtusy} are split by operating condition: models train on one operating condition (37.5\,Hz, 11\,kN) and evaluate on another (40\,Hz, 10\,kN), so speed and load both change (cross-condition), with additive white Gaussian noise applied at matched SNR to training and evaluation ($\{$clean, $+10$, $+6$, $0$, $-2$, $-6$, $-10\,\mathrm{dB}\}$). The task is binary fault classification (inner-race vs.\ outer-race dominant classes; class imbalance makes IR the hard class, Section~\ref{sec:results:perclass}). All arms share an identical training budget (50 epochs, 5 seeds; the per-class grid of Section~\ref{sec:results:perclass} used 20 epochs), identical splits, and identical evaluation labels, verified by a hash of the label vector: every reported cell carries $\mathtt{eval\_sha} = \mathtt{6c20b367\ldots}$ (\ref{app:fairness}), recomputed independently during adjudication. The hash certifies the label vector, not the input windows. In total the study comprises 865 cells across five pre-specified grids (baseline grid 475; frozen-selectivity 105; component ablation 175; graft 35; per-class 75), and four follow-up grids add 255 cells (Sections~\ref{sec:results:fullfreeze}, \ref{sec:results:sensitivity}, \ref{sec:results:graftwm}, \ref{sec:results:nativeadd} and~\ref{sec:results:reverse}).

\textbf{Composition of the data.} Training uses the bearings of the 37.5\,Hz/11\,kN condition whose failure mode is inner race or outer race (Bearing2\_1 is labelled IR; Bearing2\_2, 2\_4 and 2\_5 are labelled OR); evaluation uses those of the 40\,Hz/10\,kN condition (Bearing3\_3 and 3\_4 are IR; Bearing3\_1 and 3\_5 are OR). Bearings with cage or mixed failure modes are excluded. Windows are non-overlapping segments of 2048 samples of the horizontal channel taken from the fault phase, which is delimited by a kurtosis/RMS onset criterion with a last-15\% fallback; the evaluation set holds 3{,}504 OR and 1{,}744 IR windows, the training set 4{,}624 OR and 624 IR windows. Two consequences matter for interpretation. The IR class is learned from a single training bearing, so class-level results may reflect bearing-specific generalisation. And the five seeds resample initialisation, batch order and noise but not bearings: there are four independent bearings on each side, and none of the intervals reported here captures bearing-level uncertainty. One follow-up grid (Section~\ref{sec:results:reverse}) reverses the transition (40\,Hz/10\,kN $\rightarrow$ 37.5\,Hz/11\,kN). It uses the same eight bearings with their roles swapped, so it tests the other direction rather than independent bearings; its evaluation set holds 4{,}624 OR and 624 IR windows, all IR windows coming from Bearing2\_1, and its label hash is $\mathtt{93424834\ldots}$. The 35\,Hz/12\,kN condition has no inner-race bearing and cannot form a binary pair.

\subsection{What the frozen arm freezes, and what the checks verify}
\label{sec:protocol:freeze}

In the Mamba-3 layer used here, one input projection emits the gate and value streams, the scan inputs $B$ and $C$, the step-size and decay terms ($\Delta$ and $A$, through input-dependent additive terms), a trapezoidal weight and rotation angles. The frozen arm removes the input-dependent parts of $\Delta$, $A$, $B$ and $C$ and replaces them with learned constants broadcast over time (for $\Delta$ the static bias of the original layer is kept; for $A$ a new constant parameter plays the role of a static log-decay, passed through the layer's own heavy-tailed clamp). The removed projections are deleted, so no parameter or gradient can leak. \textbf{The trapezoidal weight and the rotation angles remain functions of the current input}, as do the stem and the output gate. The frozen arm is therefore not a time-invariant scan: in the Mamba-3 formulation~\cite{lahoti2026mamba3} the input-dependent rotation and trapezoidal weighting also enter the state update. Section~\ref{sec:results:falsify} is accordingly a statement about the classical selectivity terms $\Delta, A, B, C$ only. Three further arms close that gap by the same device: \emph{frozen$-$trap} replaces the trapezoidal weight by a learned per-head constant, \emph{frozen$-$rot} replaces the rotation angles by learned constants (so the rotation becomes a learned position-only phase rather than a function of the current input), and \emph{frozen$-$all} does both, leaving \textbf{no tensor entering the scan dependent on the input}. In each case the corresponding \texttt{in\_proj} output slice is deleted. Section~\ref{sec:results:fullfreeze} reports them. For these arms the construction check adds the decisive probe: the tensors handed to the scan are captured for two different inputs through the same module, and a tensor the arm claims to have frozen is required to be bitwise identical across them while a tensor left selective is required to differ (all three arms pass). No kernel modification is needed, because the scan interface accepts these tensors regardless of their origin.

Single-variable discipline was checked by key-level state-dictionary diffs between two models built under the same seed. For the frozen arm the diff verifies that every non-target tensor has the same name and shape as in BM3; for the component-ablation and graft arms it additionally compares the values of non-target tensors at initialisation. Every differing key is mapped to an entry in an architecture map. We do not claim byte-level identity of non-target tensors in the frozen arm, and trained weights naturally differ between arms; the checks concern how the arms were constructed.

\subsection{Reporting rule}
\label{sec:protocol:report}

Every cell is trained for a fixed budget of 50 epochs with a cosine learning-rate schedule that anneals to zero, and the macro-F1 reported in the main text is that of the \emph{final epoch}, a rule that uses no evaluation data to choose a model. The original grids recorded instead the maximum over epochs of macro-F1 on the evaluation split (the harness defines no separate validation split), and the thresholds pre-specified for those grids were written against that rule. Every arm of the chain was therefore re-run on the adjudication band ($0$, $-2$, $-6$\,dB) with the original training code, the same evaluation fingerprint and per-epoch evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:results:sensitivity}). Adjudications are reported under the rule they were specified for and under the final-epoch rule, and best-epoch values are given as a sensitivity analysis. Levels outside the band (clean, $+10$, $+6$, $-10$\,dB) and the per-class grid were not re-run; values there are best-epoch and labelled auxiliary. Because the final epoch of the same evaluation condition has been inspected repeatedly during this study, final-epoch values are not a fresh confirmatory test either.

\subsection{Confound elimination}
\label{sec:protocol:confounds}

Before component attribution, three candidate confounds were examined on the same grid. \textbf{Capacity:} S4D-wide ($1.75\times$ parameters) tracks S4D (S4D-wide $-$ S4D: $-0.4$, $-1.0$, $+0.4$\,pp at $0$, $-2$, $-6$\,dB under final-epoch reporting, all intervals including zero), while BM3-frozen exceeds S4D-wide by $+15.9$, $+20.8$, $+16.0$\,pp with intervals excluding zero at all three levels, so this particular increase in S4D's parameter count does not explain the gap; the control does not address the change in channel mixing that gate removal also entails. \textbf{Normalisation:} both SSM arms use LayerNorm and contain no BatchNorm; the arm that degrades under noise is the one \emph{without} any batch statistics, which argues against BatchNorm statistics mismatch as the explanation. Other normalisation inside the Mamba-3 layer was not varied. \textbf{Auxiliary physics loss:} an exploratory kinematics-alignment regulariser carried by the original model contributes $-0.1\pp$ pointwise (curves coincide); all attribution arms are therefore built on the pure cross-entropy variant.

\subsection{Pre-specification and adjudication}
\label{sec:protocol:prereg}

Every adjudicated question in this study had numeric decision thresholds written to a task card and to disk before the corresponding grid was launched. This is internal pre-specification, not external registration: the supporting evidence is local file modification times and run-directory names (released, \ref{app:fairness}), which are not tamper-evident and are not preserved by a repository checkout, so the trail lets readers inspect the order of events but cannot prove it. The exception is the last three follow-up grids (Sections~\ref{sec:results:graftwm}--\ref{sec:results:sensitivity}): their thresholds, and for the last two grids the title decision that depends on their outcome, were committed to the public repository before the first cell was run, which gives those grids an external time-stamp. Later questions (component ablation, graft) were specified after earlier grids had been seen, as is natural in a staged design. Adjudication was mechanical: an analysis pass applies the thresholds and emits a decision artifact; an independent reviewer pass re-computes aggregates, verifies fingerprints row-by-row, and must cite file paths and modification times for every check. The pre-specified thresholds were binding in practice: two attempted post-hoc criterion substitutions and one stale-artifact false verification were caught and re-adjudicated under the original rules --- this is the one place where pre-specification actually bit. \ref{app:incidents} details the incidents, the automated executor/reviewer implementation, and the human adjudication gates; all scientific decisions --- thresholds, rulings, and interpretations --- were made by the authors and are timestamped. Statistical tests (paired, per-condition) are reported throughout but were never adjudication criteria. A single repeat of one 2-epoch configuration in a separate process differed by $\sim\!0.7\pp$ macro-F1 (non-deterministic kernel reductions; \ref{app:confusion}); this is an observed repeat difference, not an estimated variability bound, and arm-level contrasts are judged against seed-level variability (2--9$\pp$).
