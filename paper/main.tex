\title{What Carries Deep-Noise Robustness in a Mamba-3 Block? Input-Independent Scans and Noise-Dependent Gating under Cross-Condition Shift}

\author[lsu]{Yi Wang\corref{cor1}}
\ead{jefflsxy@lsu.edu.cn}
\author[lyu]{Yongqing Tang}
\address[lsu]{College of Engineering, Lishui University, Lishui, Zhejiang 323000, People's Republic of China}
\address[lyu]{School of Physics, Mechanics and Electronics, Longyan University, Longyan, Fujian, People's Republic of China}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
The robustness of Mamba-style selective state-space models is often attributed to input-dependent selectivity. We test this with controlled ablations of one Mamba-3 block design (instantiated as a four-layer stack) on bearing vibration data under cross-condition shift (XJTU-SY, one speed-and-load pair run in both directions, training and evaluation bearings disjoint), with matched-noise training and final-epoch macro-F1 under a fixed budget. All central contrasts were run with training and evaluation noise drawn independently. Blocks whose scan dynamics and read/write coefficients are made input-independent still outperform a time-invariant S4D (this is not a claim that selectivity contributes nothing or that full performance is preserved relative to the unfrozen block): on the original transition the fully frozen block leads by $+21.3$, $+23.3$, $+20.3$\,pp at $0$, $-2$ and $-6$\,dB, and on the reverse transition by $+27.5$, $+26.7$, $+21.1$\,pp. Removing the multiplicative output gate costs $+5.4$, $+9.3$, $+32.6$\,pp in the fully frozen block, resolved only at $-6$\,dB; only in this host and direction does the cost grow significantly with noise depth, and without its gate the block still exceeds S4D at $0$ and $-2$\,dB but not at $-6$\,dB. Multiplicative gating exceeds an additive branch of identical size by $+23.3$\,pp at $-6$\,dB. On Paderborn bearings the gate-removal cost replicates when training and evaluation share bearings. Grafting a gate onto S4D, per-class results and Paderborn with disjoint bearings were run only under an earlier noise protocol and are descriptive. The evidence concerns one block design, one shift pair per dataset and five seeds.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability}
The code, pre-specification artifacts, and adjudication trail for this study are available at \url{https://github.com/jeff-1980/bm3-jizi}. The repository includes all follow-up grids (645 cells with per-epoch curves, including the 210 independent-noise cells of Sections~\ref{sec:results:indep}--\ref{sec:results:pu_shared}), their pre-specifications, drivers, construction checks and decision memos and the harness's own imported modules. The analysis can be recomputed from the released per-cell records alone (it was re-run from a fresh clone and reproduced the archived decisions byte for byte). Retraining requires the XJTU-SY and Paderborn data, a GPU and the Mamba-3 source and kernels (upstream commit \texttt{e9594ce}); a portable loader resolves the harness's imports to the repository copies, and a 2-epoch smoke run of the reverse-transition driver from a clean copy of the repository (not the author's directory tree) loaded those copies and reproduced the evaluation-label hash, but a full retraining from a clean environment has not been performed.
