\title{Gating Benefits Depend on the Backbone: An Ablation Study of a Mamba-3 Block under Matched-Noise Cross-Condition Drift}

\author{Yi Wang\corref{cor1}}
\ead{jefflsxy@lsu.edu.cn}
\address{College of Engineering, Lishui University, Lishui 323000, Zhejiang, China}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
The robustness of Mamba-style selective state-space models (SSMs) is often attributed to input-dependent selectivity. We test this attribution with a three-step ablation chain on one controlled testbed: a Mamba-3 block, bearing vibration signals (XJTU-SY, one speed-and-load transition), and noise-aware training at matched signal-to-noise ratio. Decision thresholds for each step were written down before the corresponding grid was run (internal pre-specification, not an external registry). First, replacing the input-dependent parts of $\Delta$, $A$, $B$ and $C$ by learned constants---while the input-dependent trapezoidal weights and rotation angles of the Mamba-3 scan, the convolutional stem and the output gate are kept---costs at most about 5$\pp$ under deep noise (paired intervals include zero), and the frozen block still outperforms a parameter-matched time-invariant S4D by 14--20$\pp$. Under the pre-specified criterion these four projections are therefore not the primary contributor. A second grid then freezes the trapezoidal weight and the rotation angles as well, leaving no tensor entering the scan dependent on the input: the advantage over S4D does not shrink but measures $+21.9$, $+24.0$ and $+21.2\pp$ at $0$, $-2$ and $-6$\,dB (5/5 seeds, intervals excluding zero), with fewer parameters, so the block's deep-noise advantage over a time-invariant SSM requires no input-dependent scan term at all. Second, removing the multiplicative SiLU gate from the frozen block costs 9--24$\pp$ and returns it to roughly the level of S4D. Third, adding one gate branch to S4D recovers only 17--20\% of the gap to the frozen block (the seed interval reaches the pre-specified 0.3 bound under last-epoch reporting); a host-by-gate contrast of 5--23$\pp$ indicates that, in this design and training budget, the gate's benefit depends strongly on the host. Removal and addition are not symmetric interventions, so this does not show that the gate cannot be transplanted. A separate, shorter per-class grid shows the gate-related differences concentrating on the minority inner-race class. Values are best-epoch macro-F1 on the evaluation condition; the central contrasts for four arms are re-assessed under last-epoch reporting. The evidence is conditional on one block, one condition pair with four bearings per side, five seeds and matched-noise training.
\end{abstract}
