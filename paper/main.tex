\title{Necessary in Its Native Block: Selectivity and Gating in a Mamba-3 Block under Matched-Noise Cross-Condition Drift}

\author{Yi Wang\corref{cor1}}
\ead{jefflsxy@lsu.edu.cn}
\address{College of Engineering, Lishui University, Lishui 323000, Zhejiang, China}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}

\begin{abstract}
The robustness of Mamba-style selective state-space models (SSMs) is often attributed to input-dependent selectivity. We test this attribution with a three-step ablation chain on one controlled testbed: a Mamba-3 block, bearing vibration signals (XJTU-SY, one speed-and-load transition run in both directions), and noise-aware training at matched signal-to-noise ratio. Decision thresholds for each step were written down before the corresponding grid was run; for the last grids they were first committed to a public repository. Values are macro-F1 at the final epoch of a fixed 50-epoch budget, with best-epoch values as a sensitivity analysis. First, replacing the input-dependent parts of $\Delta$, $A$, $B$ and $C$ by learned constants costs at most about $4\pp$ under deep noise (paired intervals include zero). Freezing the remaining input-dependent terms of the Mamba-3 scan as well, so that no tensor entering the scan depends on the input, leaves an advantage over a parameter-matched time-invariant S4D of $+21.9$, $+24.0$ and $+21.2\pp$ at $0$, $-2$ and $-6$\,dB (5/5 seeds, intervals excluding zero), with fewer parameters. Second, removing the multiplicative SiLU gate from the frozen block costs $9$--$23\pp$ and returns it to roughly the level of S4D. Third, replacing the gate in place by an additive branch with identical parameters and initial weights rescues none of that loss: the additive and gateless arms coincide at every level in both directions (differences within $\pm 0.6\pp$, all intervals including zero; rescue ratios $0.02$ and $0.01$, pre-specified verdict: not rescued). Adding a gate to S4D, by contrast, recovers no more than an additive branch of the same size on the original transition (30\% versus 41\% of the gap) and only a small, direction-dependent increment on the reverse transition. The input-independent scan's advantage and the cost of gate removal replicate on the reverse transition ($+22$ to $+26\pp$ and $18$--$24\pp$). Within this block the gate is necessary as a multiplicative operator, and what a time-invariant host gains from an added gate is largely the added structure. A separate per-class grid shows the gate-related differences concentrating on the minority inner-race class. The evidence is conditional on one block, one pair of operating conditions run in both directions with the same eight bearings, five seeds and matched-noise training.
\end{abstract}
